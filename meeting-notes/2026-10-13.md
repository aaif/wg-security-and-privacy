## Topics Discussed

- **Chairs update** — The first APAC meeting drew only Alex and Aaron for a 15-minute chat. The timing survey has nine responses and Monday at 9 pm Eastern is the most available slot, so Alex will move next week's APAC meeting there and keep iterating rather than give up after one attempt. He reiterated that regular attendees are not expected to join both meetings. The September monthly report was approved and merged by the TC without comment. The TC also moved the working group chair term boundary from January 1 to the anniversary of charter approval, which pushes the chair selection process later than the GitHub docs currently state.
- **Taxonomy / Landscape** — Junjie reported the initial batch of relatively straightforward terms is close to fully checked in, at roughly five to ten terms per working group, and the cross-WG group will move on to debated terms such as user and agent next. Katherine pushed an update to her pending security and privacy terms PR and will post the link in Discord for a quick review so edits land before she travels again on Sunday.
- **Threat modeling** — Fernando resumed work last week. He clarified that the first deliverable was a gap analysis of how existing methodologies such as STRIDE and PASTA hold up against systems whose behavior can change mid-flight, and the next phase is actual threat modeling that extends those methods with MITRE ATLAS, OWASP ASI, and similar lists. He will reach out to a threat modeling expert he knows about contributing now that the work is public. Alex took a moment to celebrate the completed gap analysis as one of several deliverables shipped last month.
- **Design patterns catalog** — Dan and Sagar were absent, so Alex will catch up with them offline. Aditya reported that initial drafts of three patterns merged to main a couple of days ago: approval checkpoint, attested runtime isolation, and kill switch. The next step is iterating on additional patterns and refining the drafts.
- **Best practices guide** — The first draft is out. Bar opened PRs for the pending items from the issues she created, and Fernando will review them this week after two days off. Fernando noted the guide is now open to outside contributions through the established review process and asked Alex to call that out in the next monthly report.
- **Cross-WG review checklist** — Govindaraj is still working to connect with the Agentic Commerce lead, who is taking a different approach on identity, and the observability work with Pavan and Steven is aligning from a security perspective. Feedback from Imran is being addressed. He needs to catch up with Matt on the Accuracy and Reliability collaboration, and will do so in person at AgentCon since Matt is out this week.
- **Conferences** — Katherine, Bar, Junjie, and Francesco are all presenting at MCPCon and AgentCon in San Jose, and Katherine is also at All Things Open. Manik is hosting an AAIF meetup lunch on Thursday of AgentCon and Alex encouraged the group to meet in person. Alex cannot attend because he presents at Unprompted in San Francisco the following week, where his talk argues that AI client apps need control surfaces and delegated access so security tooling can be layered on, and will point to this group's work. Katherine reported the Amsterdam meetup was small with few working groups represented. Govindaraj asked whether talks can be reserved, and Alex and Katherine explained Sked only adds sessions to a calendar while venue sizes vary widely. Katherine observed that European audiences fill rooms and stay for whole sessions far more than US audiences do.
- **LFX MCP** — Govindaraj met someone in the LF open source program who asked him to draft what the project needs from an LFX MCP. Alex shared that an early-access LFX MCP already exists and posted the community access request link, which should allow pulling AI summaries from meeting recordings. Alex will test it after the meeting, and Govindaraj will apply, noting his workstream lead status to speed approval.
- **Personal agents** — Alex raised the wave of personal agents such as Grok's bot, Meta's Muse, and OpenAI's offering, which his customers see as a data and credential exfiltration vector that moves company data outside its boundary. Muse does not support MCP natively. Instead a skill instructs it to write a skill and a Python script that invoke the MCP server with placeholder tokens, and the Sentinel gateway performs inline secret replacement as traffic leaves the cloud VM. Alex and Erin both found the pattern odd. Erin noted people reach for skills to get progressive disclosure that MCP lacks, and that MCP's new skills-as-a-resource addition does not clearly solve that either. Alex wants to probe whether the token replacement can be coaxed into leaking a real secret.
- **Cyber-capable models** — Erin shared Anthropic's opt-in vulnerability finding service for open source critical infrastructure, where repos now receive raw findings to triage themselves. Alex noted Mythos access has broadened to his employer. Fernando reported that OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google are opening cyber LLMs to individual researchers with identity verification, and Codex redirects cyber work to a Daybreak enrollment page. Muhammad hit a message saying individual verification is unavailable for his account. Alex described Daybreak as a toggle that disables newer models and moves guardrails much further out, while Fable aggressively hands off to Opus on out-of-bounds work. Erin suggested the harness matters as much as the model and that other models, including open weights, are catching up on benchmarks. Govindaraj said Worldpay is working through extensive legal paperwork to gain access.
- **Project NANDA** — Junjie followed up on Sagar's Discord post. Google works closely with Ramesh Raskar and MIT Media Lab on NANDA's open agentic web infrastructure, and a joint paper covers a verifiable trust manifest, first-party and third-party provenance governance, and discovery decoupled from runtime execution. The security angle is ensuring decentralized discovery does not create an unauthenticated attack surface. Junjie will post the paper and flagged a panel on the index launch tomorrow evening in San Carlos.
- **Attestation security analysis** — Muhammad, a researcher at TU Dresden attending his first meeting, presented a security analysis of attestation for confidentiality and integrity that found vulnerabilities, with version 2 uploaded yesterday. The work covers how attestation evidence binds to the connection carrying it and how to design protocols that preserve environment integrity. He sent it to the Security and Privacy, Identity and Trust, and Governance mailing lists on 2026-09-30 and asked where it belongs. Alex said the chairs are already working out where attestation sits across working groups and will bring this into that thread. Muhammad offered a five to ten minute presentation.
- **Agentic Commerce standards map** — Francesco, attending his first meeting and also in the Agentic Commerce WG, presents a map tomorrow of where agentic commerce standards are being developed across IETF, W3C, OpenID, AAIF, and others, covering identity, authorization, and payments. It goes public with a DOI tomorrow and he will share the link on the list. Govindaraj messaged him on Discord to set up a meeting.
- **CoSAI telemetry standard** — Victor shared the CoSAI and OpenTelemetry AI telemetry RFC built on OCSF, which he will also present at tomorrow's observability meeting, and noted CoSAI has a toolchain built with OpenSSF for manifests that is worth reviewing.
- **Onboarding** — Moushumi asked for the Discord link and mailing list access. Alex shared the working group Discord channel and the onboarding form that adds people to everything.
