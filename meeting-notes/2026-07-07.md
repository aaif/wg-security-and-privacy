## Topics Discussed

- **Monthly Technical Committee report** — Alex completed June's monthly report, now open as a PR on the repo, and July's will be covered at the next meeting. He explained the TC assigns a liaison representative who relays updates to the broader committee, framing the process as informing rather than approval, and the review window closed with no additional questions. Going forward Alex will gather a one-line update per workstream from the leads.
- **New LFX meeting scheduling** — Workstream leads now have permissions to create and schedule their own meetings on the new Linux Foundation platform. Alex asked leads to test the functionality and flag any difficulties.
- **Cross-foundation collaboration** — Alex advocated for overt alignment with groups like OWASP and AARM to strengthen the WG's leverage when asking providers such as Anthropic, OpenAI, and Cursor to adopt changes like hooks, while waiting on clearer TC guidance to stay in alignment. Fernando offered three OWASP contacts from the Agentic and AI Top 10 work. Jay White noted the WG already references other foundations' terminology through the taxonomy effort and backed an ask-forgiveness approach, and Matthew emphasized the group wants usable results over unused standards.
- **Taxonomy and Landscape review** — Junjie reported the workstream is in the execution phase, reviewing a unified working sheet built from every working group's contributions, with the goal of finishing reviews by end of week before the next Monday meeting so groups can consolidate. Katherine proposed surfacing contentious terms in the shared Discord channel before culling or redefining them, and Matthew noted the sheet is far more accessible now and easy to contribute to, having added 156 terms himself.
- **Bot and agent terminology** — Thilaknath asked how the taxonomy will distinguish bots from agents. Junjie explained that agents rely on AI and can hallucinate or behave unpredictably while "bot" is the broader term, and the group will work toward a clear definition, with members welcome to comment directly in the sheet using a name column. Sagar requested adding "Agent Card" as a term distinct from "Agent Blueprint."
- **Threat modeling** — Fernando shared an initial gap review across the eight areas identified earlier, drawing on his book research and citing MITRE ATLAS, OWASP, MAESTRO, and the NIST AI RMF as useful bases with remaining gaps to build on. He submitted the fourth deliverable and noted the final version of his book launched last week and is free to share. Alex offered to move Fernando's second and fourth deliverable documents into GitHub and post links, since Fernando is on vacation without his work laptop.
- **Design patterns catalog** — Sagar created a draft format for the catalog but was blocked from creating a GitHub branch by a permissions issue, which Alex will help resolve and escalate to the operations team if needed. Sagar has no contributors yet beyond the two leads and invited others to join.
- **Best practices guide** — Fernando delivered the first draft and asked members to review it for gaps. Matthew will set up bi-weekly meetings for the workstream once vacations end.
- **Cross-Working Group review checklist** — Govindaraj's scheduling poll drew a single vote and closed, so he will repost it, and he is reviewing other groups' materials to assemble the checklist. He asked for more proactive engagement on Discord and Slack; Alex explained the Slack-Discord bridge mirrors messages aside from emoji reactions and that the new LFX platform helps locate members' contact details, while the Discord MCP is not yet available.
- **News You Can Use** — Alex noted Fable is back, though reportedly constrained by new safety guardrails. Matthew flagged attestations coming to MCP to prevent rug-pull attacks by signing the called tool so it cannot be swapped between runs, which prompted detailed questions from Ty Smith about attestation format, metadata shape, key verification, and provenance and timestamps tied to observability and traceability. Matthew also mentioned a threat-modeling LLM benchmark and HarmBench. Hsiao-Ying Lin shared updates from the ETSI TC SAI standards meeting in Sofia, where she introduced the AAIF Security and Privacy WG and the group discussed covering AI agents in European AI Act standardization, and from the Machina and RAISE summits in Paris focused on physical AI and security.
- **Potential FINOS overlap** — Govindaraj raised possible overlap with the Linux Foundation's FINOS AI governance framework, AI reference architecture library, and AI readiness work, noting he lacks access as a non-member while companies like Oracle and Google participate. Alex will pursue access through AAIF leadership and suggested working the overlap through public channels and the TC and operations team rather than relying on the TC to deconflict.
