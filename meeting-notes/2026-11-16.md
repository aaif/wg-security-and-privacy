## Topics Discussed

- **Welcome and introductions** — Nick Fujita joined from Tokyo and Yaya Xia joined from Shanghai, both for the first time, and Matthew Khouzam stayed up late to join Alex. Nick works at [Spellguard](https://spellguard.ai/), a startup that joined AAIF as a silver member about a month ago and builds sandboxing, policy engines, guard models, and credential management. Yaya works at Ant Group, the company behind Alipay, which joined AAIF last month and maintains Kata Containers, and Yaya is focused on security and trust for agent payments and agentic commerce. Matthew connected Nick with Govindaraj on Discord during the meeting.
- **Why an APAC meeting** — Alex confirmed that the APAC session is a standing commitment made to AAIF CTO Manik at AgentCon Tokyo in September, after APAC attendees asked for a working-hours path into the working groups. The goal is a self-sustaining group in this slot that hands work back and forth with the primary meeting through notes and Discord, so the working group makes progress every week across alternating time zones. Matthew added in chat that the group should not exclude 33 to 40 percent of the world's brainpower and still assume it is making a standard.
- **Working group overview** — Alex walked the new members through the five deliverables, and Matthew summarized them in chat with a password analogy: taxonomy defines what makes a password a password, best practices says to use a strong and unique one, design patterns shows how to create and use it, the cross-WG checklist gets everyone using one, and the threat model explains what happens when someone gets yours. Matthew described the plan to turn the best practices guide into static analysis rules for a deterministic CI loop and to package threat models as skills. Alex liked that the CI loop checks every PR automatically, and Matthew called it a hard counter to slop. Yaya asked where the group's outputs live, and Alex pointed to GitHub for durable versioned work, Discord for conversation and coordination, the mailing list for meeting notes, and the Slack bridge for anyone who cannot use Discord.
- **Control surfaces PR and the Agent Harness group** — Nick noticed that [PR #45](https://github.com/aaif/wg-security-and-privacy/pull/45) has sat for three weeks, asked how the group iterates on drafts, and offered to help review. Alex explained that the PR grew out of the Unprompted talk in San Francisco and now needs a home, since a proposed Agent Harness working group opened its Discord channel on 2026-11-16 with a scope that lines up directly with it. Nick could not see the Agent Harness Discussion channel because it is private, so Matthew shared its contents with Nick directly and had already flagged the issue to Dawn. Alex will follow up with Dawn on why it is the only private channel.
- **Charter review and working group realignment** — Alex recapped the 2026-11-10 discussion on revisiting the charters six months after approval. Matthew favors a bigger step that realigns the working groups around functions, since security and privacy cuts across nearly everything, and wants it to become an AAIF proposal that anyone can carry forward. Alex credited Govindaraj's review checklist work and Matthew's attendance across many groups for keeping security and privacy connected to the rest of AAIF.
- **APAC-friendly times across working groups** — Yaya wants to join Agentic Commerce, whose meeting lands around midnight in Shanghai, and Matthew pointed Yaya to Adyen's representative in that group as a strong contact. Alex noted that the chairs have discussed APAC-friendly sessions since September, following a directive from Manik roughly two months ago, and Matthew believes this group still holds the only one. Nick would also like APAC sessions for Attestation & Evidence, which held its kickoff and just closed a scheduling poll, as well as Identity & Trust, Governance & Risk, and the proposed Agent Harness group, which has no meeting or charter yet. Alex will raise all of these at the chairs meeting on 2026-11-23 and report back.
- **Sandbox standards and security datasets** — Nick evaluates sandbox products regularly, including Open Sandbox, which Alibaba contributed to AAIF, and would welcome a sandbox standard to evaluate against along with a catalog of known vulnerabilities to check products against. Yaya noted that Ant Group works closely with the Open Sandbox team and that Open Sandbox supports isolation options such as Kata Containers and gVisor. Nick shared Spellguard's newly released [Spellguard-1.0-8B](https://huggingface.co/Spellguard/Spellguard-1.0-8B) guard model, a fine-tune of Qwen3, along with its [eval scripts](https://github.com/Spellguard/spellguard-models/tree/main/eval), and suggested the group could own an open catalog of the best security datasets. Matthew recommended nono.sh and Eclipse Enclave as a base to build on and the Accuracy and Reliability WG as a home for the benchmarks.
- **Meeting records** — Nick asked whether the meetings are recorded, and Alex explained that the LFX portal holds the recordings, transcripts, and AI summaries for every meeting while this group also publishes its own summaries in GitHub. Nick asked whether other working groups do the same, and Alex expects consistency to improve soon now that the chairs share skills for meeting notes and monthly reports in a new chairs repo.
- **Getting involved** — Nick asked where contributions are most wanted, and Matthew and Alex both encouraged contributing to whatever sparks interest, since this work happens outside everyone's day job and should stay fun enough to avoid burnout. Matthew added that shyness is the enemy in a field moving this fast, so half-formed questions are welcome early and often.
- **Scheduling** — The next primary meeting is 2026-11-24 and the next APAC meeting is Monday 2026-11-30.
