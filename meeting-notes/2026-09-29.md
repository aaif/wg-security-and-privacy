## Topics Discussed

- **Chairs update** — Alex returned from speaking in Tokyo and missed the last meeting while in flight. The chairs discussed delegating PR approval beyond Alex and Junjie so workstream leads can approve and merge, balanced against the influx of AI-generated PRs now that the repos are public, and they are drafting an AI contribution policy. GitHub settings changes still route through Christina.
- **AI-generated drive-by PRs** — Matthew reached out on Discord to an individual who had opened more than 75 PRs across random AAIF repos, advised them to pick one or two repos and build relationships since the work is about community rather than code, and most of those PRs were subsequently dropped. Sal proposed accepting AI-drafted first contributions, which often come from contributors working outside the project's dominant language, as long as the follow-up response to a maintainer is human. Alex relayed the chairs' preference that contributors discuss a problem in a meeting before opening a PR, since drive-by PRs usually identify a real issue and implement it wrong, and Katherine noted human drive-bys happen too.
- **AI-assisted review** — Matthew requested the group adopt CodeRabbit or a similar AI reviewer to give a first pass on incoming PRs and immediate feedback to contributors, using AI to counter AI-generated volume.
- **Review cycle time** — Matthew's timing analysis shows authoring takes little time, waiting for review takes most of it, and the review itself is short. A fix for the broken taxonomy build took three weeks because each comment round trip took about three days, and he has since added strict CI so the build cannot break again. He called review by begging and nagging unsustainable and argued reviewing should be more glorified than authoring. Sal suggested defining four goals for community contributors by year end and over-communicating governance directly in the PR and issue flow, drawing on how Kubernetes signals what is open for contribution and when.
- **Review notifications** — Alex disabled GitHub email notifications and normally relies on Slack subscriptions, which are not enabled for the AAIF org, so he has been manually checking for review requests and apologized for the resulting delay. He asked to be pinged on Discord when reviews stall, and shared that his company uses a review-ratio leaderboard to incentivize reviewing. Matthew's personal rule is to get one contribution from someone else merged for every one he lands.
- **APAC meeting** — Alex proposed an alternate-week meeting for the time zones the current 10 am Pacific slot excludes, which lands at 2 am in Japan. The group converged on 10 pm Eastern, which is 9 pm Central and 8-9 am in Japan, and Fernando noted it becomes 11 pm for him after the US time change since Brazil does not shift. Sal advised that the first split is the hardest, that each session needs at least six weeks of one or more consistent attendees, and that one session should hold the central notes pointing to PRs and issues so asynchronous follow-up stays fast. Sal offered to attend both meetings for the first six weeks, and Alex will attend both as the bridge between them.
- **Cross-region collaboration** — Govindaraj suggested LFX could seek funding from AWS, Google, or Microsoft for translation services that would benefit all LFX initiatives, and proposed pairing each workstream lead with a peer lead in the APAC region so both sides drive the same goals. Sal noted nobody in this time zone can pick the right APAC time, so the proposal should go to Discord for the affected contributors to answer.
