## Topics Discussed

- **Public access to past meetings** — Junjie reported that all Discord channels, GitHub repositories, and meetings are now public, and asked whether past meeting recordings should also be opened up. The group saw no confidential content and supported defaulting to open, and Mirja noted that attendees should be informed since the meetings were originally announced as closed.
- **Upcoming conferences** — Junjie attends DEF CON in Vegas this week and joins a roundtable on the open, autonomous agentic web. Alex speaks at an Agent and MCP Con in September, and Junjie and Katherine both speak at AgentCon / MCPCon in San Jose in October.
- **Agentic AI Summit recap** — Junjie and Erin attended sessions from the past weekend's summit. Attendees there are already reviewing this WG's best practices guide and gap analysis PRs, and a memorable framing was that agents perform actions while humans remain accountable and responsible.
- **Agent accountability and cross-WG collaboration** — Fernando bridged the accountability discussion to the Identity and Authorization workstream so that group owns the identity portions of the best practices guide. Spence, new to AAIF, described his work on human attribution for every agent action and tool call, including service account cases, plus the observability and governance needed for a clear audit trail. Junjie proposed joint or focused cross-WG workstreams for the overlapping areas and will raise it in the chairs meeting.
- **Taxonomy / Landscape** — Katherine flagged that the security and privacy terms in the unified spreadsheet are close to needing this group's review, which will happen through PRs. The workstream now meets weekly with sessions often running long, covers roughly 10-15 terms per meeting, and is deferring overly general terms so the first batch stays agent-specific, with "agent" itself still the hardest term to define.
- **Threat modeling** — Fernando's PR with the initial gap analysis against OWASP, MITRE ATLAS, MAESTRO, and NIST AI RMF is under review, and he will address comments by end of next week. He is also evaluating how STRIDE and PASTA align with agentic AI threat modeling, and Alan Blount of Google left feedback suggesting coverage of UI/HUA considerations and the MCP specification released the prior week.
- **PR acceptance process** — Fernando asked how PRs get accepted into the AAIF repository. Junjie clarified that the working group can review and merge without waiting for TC approval since the work is within the approved charter, and the TC sees merged work through monthly reports and can raise questions.
- **Design patterns catalog** — Aditya reported that Sagar posted an initial draft and a template for adding patterns, with a PR to the AAIF GitHub repo still to come before broader review.
- **Best practices guide** — Fernando submitted a first skeleton draft of the Agentic AI Best Practices Guide covering high-level topics and identified gaps, including identity and authorization content that requires coordination with the other group. Junjie left comments on both PRs, and the group agreed GitHub PRs work well once documents mature, with Google Docs remaining useful for early brainstorming.
- **Cross-WG review checklist** — Govindaraj held the workstream's first meeting with the four group leads and assigned action items, starting with a gap analysis of documents to identify overlaps and avoid stepping on other working groups' scope. He is waiting on Tracy for permission to update GitHub issues and assignees, and is tracking work in a spreadsheet in the interim.
- **News and open discussion** — Aditya flagged another npm supply chain attack with compromised packages carrying the same malware seen previously. Junjie raised a new "agent plugin" standard and will share the link, and Erin's research into agent discovery approaches (DNS-based methods, agent catalogs, AID) prompted discussion of Project NANDA from MIT, zero-trust verification of published agents, and the interoperability work needed to avoid conflicting standards.

## Decisions

- Past working group meetings will be made public by default, since no confidential or proprietary information was discussed, with any concerns to be raised with Alex and Junjie.
- The working group can merge PRs after group review without waiting for TC approval, since the deliverables fall within the approved charter and the TC reviews progress through monthly reports.
