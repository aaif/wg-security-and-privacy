Matthew suggested in chat that a combined session could eventually split into infrastructure, applications, and compliance tracks, and Charna Parkey liked the idea.

Matthew added that now is the best time to open a PR and get it positively dogpiled by engineers with strong opinions. Alex noted that [PR #45](https://github.com/aaif/wg-security-and-privacy/pull/45) went up in the same spirit, since a concrete draft gets people with strong opinions to start the conversation. Junjie joined late after the workstream round.

Alex wondered whether publishing the first deliverable gave newcomers something to iterate on, and Matthew suspects a new wave of member companies. Matthew summed up the current phase as the "let's get stuff done" phase, which is also the fun one.
- **Cross-WG review checklist and Dallas event** — Govindaraj was absent, and Matthew noted in chat that Monday's checklist session was cancelled. Alex shared that Govindaraj's January community event in Dallas has grown well beyond the original pitch, with AAIF, several organizations, and multiple universities now involved. Alex encouraged everyone to help promote it once more details land and plans to lobby Govindaraj to stream it online.
- **Welcome and introductions** — Luis Saiz joined for the first time from BBVA, where Luis leads security innovation during a large AI transformation and contributes to personal agent design work. Francesco Marinoni Moretto joined from Milan, where Francesco advises a major banking institution and sees banks needing to support know-your-agent practices. Francesco noted that new Wells Fargo customer rules take effect today, which make customers fully responsible for their agents' actions and require them to revoke agents by phone or postal mail. Gurvinder Singh added in chat that Bank of America and United have published similar AI use rules.
- **HOL standards map and benchmarks** — Ty Smith has been deep in production security incident work as CVE volumes climb, and shared several HOL updates. CIGAR 14.2 shipped with an [agnostic context graph benchmark](https://github.com/hashgraph-online/context-graph-benchmark) that compares three context graphs on security, privacy, and efficiency measures such as token usage, and Ty is working to host the Agent Action Capsule demo on the HOL site. Ty also released a [standards map](https://hol.org/standards/map) that charts standards bodies, their working groups, and their standards, lets you compare any two of them, and ships with an open source MCP server so an agent can analyze the map. Ty sees it helping AAIF find which bodies to engage, spot coverage gaps, and line up fields across standards for interoperability, and shared an [AAIF-focused view](https://hol.org/standards/map#map?entity=aaif). Francesco had emailed Ty about combining the HOL map with Francesco's own Agentic standards map, and Ty will respond and set up a half-hour session early next week to map AAIF's internal working groups into an internal tool or MCP server.
- **Agentic Commerce gaps document** — Francesco sent the document Govindaraj requested last week, which lists what each AAIF working group is missing for Agentic Commerce, and asked whether it should go out as a PR or to the mailing list. Matthew and Alex both recommended a PR in this group's repository, since comments, incremental changes, linked issues, and follow-up PRs are all easy to track there.
- **Workstream meetings explained** — Monique Tronchin asked how the checklist, best practices, and primary meetings differ. Alex explained that the checklist and best practices sessions are workstream meetings within this group that focus on their own deliverables, and Francesco clarified that those sessions are recorded on LFX and open for anyone to join. This group publishes notes for the primary and APAC meetings only. Matthew described the checklist as requirements that keep everyone aware of existing work and surface gaps, and best practices as the specs for solving those problems. Matthew's chat version is the one worth framing: the checklist says "stop re-inventing stuff," best practices says "this is how you do stuff," and this meeting says "let's be secure."
- **Universal AAIF calendar** — Matthew asked again for a universal AAIF calendar spanning every working group and project, including Goose, MCP, and A2A, so overlaps become obvious, such as the Accuracy and Reliability meeting colliding with A2A. Francesco shared the [LFX meetings page](https://zoom-lfx.platform.linuxfoundation.org/meetings/agentic-ai-foundation) and agreed the calendar should come with an MCP server. Alex noted that the existing LFX MCP covers only the LFX dashboard and has been advocating for all AAIF sources to be available to agents, and Alex will add the calendar request to the monthly report for the TC.
- **Discord and meeting records** — Luis cannot reach Discord from a corporate laptop and asked whether to request an exception. Matthew said Discord is as active as you want it to be, Francesco suggested the mobile app, and Matthew shared that interesting Zoom chat lands in Discord afterward as a searchable archive. Alex suggested pointing an agent at the workstream recordings to generate notes that improve on the Zoom AI summaries, and Matthew noted that much of that work is still infrastructure for building things and not yet noteworthy.
- **News You Can Use** — Erin Farr raised Artex, an open source penetration testing framework that its author pulled after attackers adopted it, which prompted an [FBI flash advisory](https://www.ic3.gov/CSA/2026/261113.pdf) and [renewed press coverage](https://www.cnet.com/tech/services-and-software/hackers-artex-ai-cyberattacks-fbi-warning/). The claim behind the attention is that Artex achieves Mythos-level impact by orchestrating lesser-known models, and it was reportedly used against a bank in Asia. Alex noted that vendors are already writing detection signatures for AI pen testing frameworks and relayed a researcher's view that a swarm of inexpensive models can find the same issues as guardrailed frontier models. Erin agreed that the harness drives much of the impact, and Gurvinder reported at least four similar autonomous frameworks, called detection whack-a-mole for blue teams, and will summarize CrowdStrike's answer on detecting them in Discord. Matthew also shared IBM's [Adversarial Robustness Toolbox](https://research.ibm.com/projects/adversarial-robustness-toolbox).
- **Vulnerability research and supply chain** — Gurvinder manages a small team that covers both red and blue work, researches MCP security, and asked who else works on incident response. Filipe Rodrigues identified as purple, and Muhammad Usama Sardar described using formal analysis to find attacks and propose fixes, with roughly 80 CVEs expected in total against the early attestation ecosystem, with 20 published and 57 or more still processing, summarized in their [current draft](https://www.ietf.org/archive/id/draft-intra-handshake-fail-65.html#section-1.1). Gurvinder has been mapping the recent Railway attack to the OpenSSF Scorecard to produce simple guidance for securing deployment pipelines, and Muhammad will get in touch to collaborate. Matthew pointed to the OpenSSF supply chain attack graph in the Best Practices Guide Discord channel as a reminder that every node and edge in a dependency graph can be attacked, and Erin added that everyone along the chain is overwhelmed, including the people applying patches.
- **Holiday outlook** — The group expects a busy December for blue teams with so many people out, and Jeff Borek recalled when Log4j was the only thing that ruined a December. Matthew closed by teaching everyone the "turkey" fist bump for annoying children over the holiday and requested stories of success next week.
