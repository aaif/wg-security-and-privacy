# [Report] Security & Privacy – August 2026

**Working Group:** WG Security & Privacy

**Reporting period:** August 2026

**Chair(s):** Alexander Frazer (Runlayer) & Junjie Bu (Google)

**Date submitted:** 2026-08-30

---

## Summary

In August the working group opened its doors and its review queues. Past meeting
recordings will now be public by default, and all three active drafts (Threat
Modeling Gap Analysis, Best Practices Guide, Design Patterns Catalog) moved through
review or toward PR. Cross-WG coordination expanded on several fronts, including the
review checklist workstream, a proposed shared protocols effort, and the taxonomy term
PRs now heading to this group for review.

## Progress Against Objectives

- **Agentic AI Threat Modeling Gap Analysis and Framework Design** (Alon Mazor, Fernando
  Lucktemberg) — The gap analysis PR covering OWASP, MITRE ATLAS, MAESTRO, and the NIST
  AI RMF is in active review. Fernando is addressing comments, evaluating how STRIDE and
  PASTA align with agentic AI threat modeling, and incorporating feedback from Alan
  Blount (Google) suggesting coverage of UI/HUA considerations and the newly released
  MCP specification. Bi-weekly workstream meetings are being set up.
- **Agentic AI Security Best Practices Guide** (Fernando Lucktemberg, Matthew Khouzam) —
  Fernando submitted a skeleton draft PR identifying key topics and gaps, with identity
  and authorization content owned by the Identity and Authorization group and HUA
  coverage planned for the next revision. The workstream also reviewed Erin Farr's
  security best practices guide, which she committed to submitting as a PR. Review of
  Fernando's contributed book resumes when he returns from vacation.
- **Security and Privacy Design Patterns Catalog** (Dan Kommatas, Sagar Dashora) — Sagar
  posted an initial catalog draft and a template for adding patterns, with a PR to the
  AAIF GitHub repository to follow before broader review.
- **Cross-Working Group Security and Privacy Review Checklist** (Govindaraj Palanisamy)
  — The workstream held its first two meetings with the four group leads and started the
  overlap checklist between groups. The analysis surfaced gaps where other WGs' notes
  and Git content are unclear, so Govindaraj is requesting chair contacts to confirm
  scope and deliverables directly. The meeting time poll is being re-run after
  scheduling conflicts.
- **Taxonomy / Landscape** (cross-WG, delegates Jay White and Katherine Druckman) — The
  first pass of the easier terms is nearly complete and duplicate-term conflicts across
  working groups are resolved. The workstream meets weekly and covers roughly 10-15
  terms per session, deferring overly general terms so the first batch stays
  agent-specific. PRs with the security and privacy terms will come to this WG for
  review over the coming weeks, with results expected by end of month or early next.

## Ecosystem Highlights

- OWASP released its updated Top 10 for Agentic systems.
- FINOS Labs launched the Open Enterprise Agent Governance and Orchestration project,
  covering a governance and compliance control plane for A2A, MCP, and related
  protocols, which may overlap with this WG's governance work.
- Attendees at the Agentic AI Summit are already reviewing this WG's best practices
  guide and gap analysis PRs. Junjie joined a DEF CON roundtable on the open, autonomous
  agentic web.
- The group discussed OpenAI's Black Hat talk on their sandbox escape incident,
  observing that the presentation downplayed the design flaws (inter-agent communication
  and uncontrolled egress) that enabled the escape.
- Aditya flagged another npm supply chain attack with compromised packages carrying the
  same malware seen previously.
- The Identity and Trust WG requested this group's feedback on a draft framework for
  cross-boundary observability in agentic systems, routed through the review checklist
  workstream.
- Members present at AgentCon / MCPCon in San Jose in October, including a workshop on
  the OpenSSF Secure Agentic Framework MCP skill, and Alex speaks in Tokyo in September.

## Blockers and Risks

- Overlapping ownership across working groups remains unresolved. Multiple groups
  sometimes claim the same areas, and the A2A donation raises new scope questions for
  protocol-related work.
- General availability remains a standing risk. Black Hat and DEF CON drew a large share
  of the membership during the first week of August, as anticipated in the July report.

## Decisions Needed from the TC

- The WG requests clarification on how the A2A donation affects working group scopes,
  particularly for protocol-related security work spanning this group, Agent Gateway,
  and other AAIF efforts.

## Next Month's Focus
