# [Report] Security & Privacy – October 2026

**Working Group:** WG Security & Privacy

**Reporting period:** October 2026

**Chair(s):** Alexander Frazer (Runlayer) & Junjie Bu (Google)

**Date submitted:** 2026-10-30

---

## Summary

October was the month after the shipping month. The Best Practices Guide is now open to
outside contributions through the established review process, and contributors answered
with seven issues and six pull requests against it in the first three weeks. The Security
and Privacy terms merged into the cross-WG taxonomy on 2026-10-26, threat modeling resumed
after the gap analysis, and the WG launched its alternate-week APAC meeting, now settled
on Mondays at 9 pm Eastern. The TC approved the September report without comment, and the
month closed with AAIF working to align its seven to eight working groups into five
focused groups, with security and privacy treated as a cross-cutting function.

  Charter target 2026-10-01, met in September. The

- **Security and Privacy Design Patterns Catalog** (Dan Kommatas, Sagar Dashora) —
  Charter target 2026-10-01, met in September. The
  [catalog landing page](https://github.com/aaif/wg-security-and-privacy/blob/main/workstreams/design-patterns/DRAFT-design_patterns_catalog.md)

  [Attested Isolated Runtime](https://github.com/aaif/wg-security-and-privacy/blob/main/workstreams/design-patterns/DRAFT-attested-isolated-runtime.md)

  the leads will triage. On 2026-10-27 Marc Molas Carol presented a kill switch
  revocation protocol with three revocation paths, and the group pointed him at OpenID
  CAEP, the Shared Signals Framework, and RFC 7009 token revocation as alignment targets,
  which gives the Kill Switch pattern a concrete protocol-level input. Memory-related
  patterns remain the next planned additions.
- **Cross-Working Group Security and Privacy Review Checklist** (Govindaraj Palanisamy)
  — Charter target 2026-06-01, no v0 draft yet. Observability alignment is progressing
  with Pavan and Steven, Agentic Commerce involvement advanced through in-person meetings
  at AgentCon and Francesco Marinoni Moretto's published standards map, and Govindaraj is
  connecting with the Governance group so a representative joins the WG. On 2026-10-27 he
  presented a proposal to regroup the working groups around the AAIF projects and to run
  feedback loops between projects through GitHub workflows, which he will share on
  Discord. Workflow and Integration still lacks a delegate.

  Katherine revised the Security and Privacy terms in cross-WG PR #56 on 2026-10-13,
  separating agent sabotage from mishandling/misuse by actor authority and removing named
  technologies from privacy-preserving execution. The PR merged on 2026-10-26, which puts
  the WG's security and privacy terms into the shared AAIF taxonomy. The first round of
  terms is nearing completion across working groups, with debate continuing on contested
  terms such as user and agent.

## Ecosystem Highlights

- Katherine, Bar, Junjie, and Francesco presented at or attended MCPCon and AgentCon in
  San Jose, Katherine also spoke at All Things Open, and Manik hosted an AAIF meetup lunch
  during AgentCon. Alex presents at \[un\]prompted in San Francisco the following week on why AI
  client apps need control surfaces and delegated access so security tooling can be
  layered on, pointing to this WG's work.
- Personal agents such as Grok's bot, Meta's Muse, and OpenAI's offering are showing up
  as a data and credential exfiltration vector that moves company data outside its
  boundary. Muse does not support MCP natively. A skill instructs it to write a skill and
  a Python script that invoke the MCP server with placeholder tokens, and the Sentinel
  gateway performs inline secret replacement as traffic leaves the cloud VM. Alex wants
  to probe whether the token replacement can be coaxed into leaking a real secret, and
  Erin noted people reach for skills to get the progressive disclosure MCP lacks.
- Cyber-capable models are opening up. Anthropic launched an opt-in vulnerability finding
  service for open source critical infrastructure where repositories triage raw findings
  themselves, and OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google are opening cyber LLMs to individual
  researchers with identity verification. Codex redirects cyber work to a Daybreak
  enrollment page, one member found individual verification unavailable for his account,
  and Worldpay is working through legal paperwork to gain access. Erin suggested the
  harness matters as much as the model and that open-weight models are catching up on
  benchmarks.
- Google works closely with Ramesh Raskar and MIT Media Lab on Project NANDA's open
  agentic web infrastructure. A joint paper covers a verifiable trust manifest,
  first-party and third-party provenance governance, and discovery decoupled from runtime
  execution. The security angle is ensuring decentralized discovery does not create an
  unauthenticated attack surface.
- Muhammad Usama Sardar of TU Dresden presented a security analysis of attestation for
  confidentiality and integrity that found vulnerabilities, covering how attestation
  evidence binds to the connection carrying it and how to design protocols that preserve
  environment integrity. He offered a short presentation to the group, and the chairs are
  folding the work into their existing thread on where attestation sits across working
  groups, including whether it becomes a cross-functional workstream or its own group.
- AAIF is working to align its seven to eight working groups into five focused groups,
  with security and privacy treated as a cross-cutting function. Two new working group
  proposals went to AAIF leadership: one on agent memory and context, judged to be
  covered by existing work, and a 90-day pilot on attestation and verification.
- Ty Smith presented attestation and verification work spanning Merkle tree security,
  decentralized trustless provability, AAC standards, and GoDaddy's ANS protocol for
  decentralized node networks. Junjie is convening Ty, Govindaraj, and Steven to review a
  combined attestation and verification proposal covering traceability, quality, safety,
  security, and identity.
- Marc Molas Carol presented the Kill Switch protocol for the MintID chain, which gives
  agents on the open internet KYC-backed identity and accountability with three
  revocation paths: a kill switch triggered by the agent or user, a cascade revocation
  across all agents under a root authority, and a master kill switch held by the issuer.
  Revocation took 28 to 33 seconds in testing. Govindaraj recommended aligning with
  OpenID's CAEP and Shared Signals Framework, RFC 7009 token revocation, and back-channel
  logout, and offered to connect Marc with people preparing an IETF draft. Marc first
  introduced the Mint ID work at the 2026-10-19 APAC meeting and offered to help gather
  APAC participants for cross-working group security alignment.
- Monique Tronchin, new to the group and active in the x402 Identity Working Group and
  the Open Secure AI Alliance, proposed a shared channel for cross-group alignment on
  definitions, taxonomy, and identity approaches.
- Govindaraj will present at an IEEE MetroCon meeting on assembling open source projects
  to solve interim problems and converge on future solutions.
- Francesco's map of agentic commerce standards across IETF, W3C, OpenID, AAIF, and
  others, covering identity, authorization, and payments, went public with a DOI.
- Victor Lu shared the CoSAI and OpenTelemetry AI telemetry RFC built on OCSF and noted
  CoSAI has a toolchain built with OpenSSF for manifests worth reviewing.
