## Summary

October was the month after the shipping month. The Best Practices Guide is now open to
outside contributions through the established review process, and contributors answered
with seven issues and six pull requests against it in the first three weeks. The Security
and Privacy taxonomy terms PR collected three approvals in the cross-WG repository and is
waiting on merge, threat modeling resumed after the gap analysis, and the WG launched its
alternate-week APAC meeting, now settled on Mondays at 9 pm Eastern.
The TC approved the September report without comment.

## Ecosystem Highlights

- Katherine, Bar, Junjie, and Francesco presented at or attended MCPCon and AgentCon in
  San Jose, Katherine also spoke at All Things Open, and Manik hosted an AAIF meetup lunch
  during AgentCon. Alex presents at \[un\]prompted in San Francisco the following week on why AI
  client apps need control surfaces and delegated access so security tooling can be
  layered on, pointing to this WG's work.
- Personal agents such as Grok's bot, Meta's Muse, and OpenAI's offering are showing up
  as a data and credential exfiltration vector that moves company data outside its
  boundary. Muse does not support MCP natively. A skill instructs it to write a skill and
  a Python script that invoke the MCP server with placeholder tokens, and the Sentinel
  gateway performs inline secret replacement as traffic leaves the cloud VM. Alex wants
  to probe whether the token replacement can be coaxed into leaking a real secret, and
  Erin noted people reach for skills to get the progressive disclosure MCP lacks.
- Cyber-capable models are opening up. Anthropic launched an opt-in vulnerability finding
  service for open source critical infrastructure where repositories triage raw findings
  themselves, and OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google are opening cyber LLMs to individual
  researchers with identity verification. Codex redirects cyber work to a Daybreak
  enrollment page, one member found individual verification unavailable for his account,
  and Worldpay is working through legal paperwork to gain access. Erin suggested the
  harness matters as much as the model and that open-weight models are catching up on
  benchmarks.
- Google works closely with Ramesh Raskar and MIT Media Lab on Project NANDA's open
  agentic web infrastructure. A joint paper covers a verifiable trust manifest,
  first-party and third-party provenance governance, and discovery decoupled from runtime
  execution. The security angle is ensuring decentralized discovery does not create an
  unauthenticated attack surface.
- Muhammad Usama Sardar of TU Dresden presented a security analysis of attestation for
  confidentiality and integrity that found vulnerabilities, covering how attestation
  evidence binds to the connection carrying it and how to design protocols that preserve
  environment integrity. He offered a short presentation to the group, and the chairs are
  folding the work into their existing thread on where attestation sits across working
  groups, including whether it becomes a cross-functional workstream or its own group.
- Francesco's map of agentic commerce standards across IETF, W3C, OpenID, AAIF, and
  others, covering identity, authorization, and payments, went public with a DOI.
- Victor Lu shared the CoSAI and OpenTelemetry AI telemetry RFC built on OCSF and noted
  CoSAI has a toolchain built with OpenSSF for manifests worth reviewing.
