# [Report] Security & Privacy – September 2026

**Working Group:** WG Security & Privacy

**Reporting period:** September 2026

**Chair(s):** Alexander Frazer (Runlayer) & Junjie Bu (Google)

**Date submitted:** 2026-09-30

---

## Summary

In September the working group shipped its first deliverables. The Best Practices Guide
and the Threat Modeling Gap Analysis merged on 2026-09-30 and are registered as Drafts in
`deliverables/`, the Design Patterns Catalog landed its first four pattern drafts, and
Erin Farr's Security Best Practices Feature Matrix merged earlier in the month. The WG's
security and privacy terms went up for review in the cross-WG taxonomy repository, the
group set merge governance rules for the `deliverables/` folder, and it agreed to trial
an alternate-week meeting for APAC members.

## Progress Against Objectives

- **Agentic AI Security Best Practices Guide** (Fernando Lucktemberg, Matthew Khouzam) —
  The WG decided to freeze, merge, and release the first version of the guide, then
  socialize it internally and promote it publicly as the group's first deliverable.
  Identity and trust topics are excluded from this version pending ownership discussion
  with the Identity and Trust WG. PR #9 drew roughly 88 review comments; the group set a
  comment cutoff, applied simple fixes directly, and converted larger items to
  referenced GitHub issues (for example issue #23 on workload identity guidance for
  multi-cloud and hybrid deployments). Fernando completed the final review pass and the
  guide merged on 2026-09-30. It is registered as a Draft deliverable at
  `deliverables/DRAFT-agentic-ai-security-best-practices-guide.md` and is now a living
  document maintained through issues and PRs, with Fernando and Matthew as mandatory
  reviewers and owners of the remaining comments asked to open issues or PRs. Erin's
  Security Best Practices Feature Matrix, which maps each practice to the people,
  process, and technology features required to support it, merged via PR #20. Erin is
  also converting the practices into rules usable by static analysis tools and
  comparing the guide against major SDK security guidance, starting with the Google
  ADK.
- **Agentic AI Threat Modeling Gap Analysis and Framework Design** (Alon Mazor, Fernando
  Lucktemberg) — PR #8 merged on 2026-09-30 as Draft v0.1 and is registered at
  `deliverables/DRAFT-agentic-ai-threat-modeling-gap-analysis.md`. The draft evaluates
  OWASP, MITRE ATLAS, CSA MAESTRO, and the NIST AI RMF across eight agentic threat areas
  and recommends adopting the existing frameworks as the WG's classification baseline
  and directing new effort at the specific gaps it identifies. Dedicated threat
  modeling sessions start the week of 2026-10-05, scheduled directly after the best
  practices session so the two workstreams interleave, and Fernando is looking for a
  morning slot so APJ members can join.
- **Security and Privacy Design Patterns Catalog** (Dan Kommatas, Sagar Dashora) — The
  kill-switch patterns were drafted and reviewed within the workstream early in the
  month, and Sagar's PR #26 merged on 2026-09-30 with the first four drafts under
  `workstreams/design-patterns/`: the catalog landing page with the required pattern
  template, Approval Checkpoint, Kill Switch, and Attested Isolated Runtime. The
  Attested Isolated Runtime draft originated as external PR #11, which closed in favour
  of #26 with its prior review carrying over. Aditya Gidh and Rul1an reviewed #26, and
  Rul1an's follow-up PR #28 on the Approval Checkpoint pattern is the first change
  proposed against the catalog. Dan has re-engaged with the workstream after several
  months away, and memory-related patterns are planned next.
- **Cross-Working Group Security and Privacy Review Checklist** (Govindaraj Palanisamy)
  — Scope overlap issues for each working group (#12 through #18) are open on the
  project board, working group leader contacts have been identified, and a cross-group
  meeting invitation with an availability poll is being sent. Security and Privacy
  members cover Identity and Trust and Observability and Tracing well, and Matthew now
  attends Accuracy and Reliability. Agentic Commerce, Governance, and Workflow and
  Integration have no representation yet, and the chairs are raising this in the chairs
  meeting. Junjie is following up with LF staff on project board permissions so issues
  can be assigned to sub-workstream leads. Govindaraj is also surveying existing LFX
  work on AI and agent security, including a FINOS project on AI threat and risk
  mitigation, so the checklist can patch gaps without recreating existing assets.
- **Taxonomy / Landscape** (cross-WG, delegates Jautau White and Katherine Druckman) —
  Katherine's PR adding the Security and Privacy terms opened in the cross-WG repository
  on 2026-09-01 and is under review, with discussion on whether "Privacy Preserving
  Execution" should also cover sensitive and confidential data beyond privacy data.
  Katherine is pushing her pending edits and has requested another review to land the
  terms. The workstream closed roughly 10-15 definitions across five PRs mid-month and
  adopted a merge-after-quiet-period process. Matthew shipped a taxonomy browser that
  lets contributors draft definitions with their own AI provider and records the LLM
  used and the human edit distance in the resulting PR. After Matthew reached out to an
  individual who had opened more than 75 AI-generated PRs across AAIF repositories,
  roughly 40 of those PRs were dropped.

## Ecosystem Highlights

- ETSI TC SAI chartered a new work item on cybersecurity requirements for AI services
  and systems, extending an existing European Norm to cover AI agents, with work
  progressing over the next year. Hsiao-Ying Lin introduced this WG's work there and
  asked whether a formal bidirectional information exchange can be established between
  the two bodies.
- OpenAI, Google, and Anthropic announced a stronger focus on testing and safety, which
  the group received with some skepticism given the pace of model releases. A member
  recounted a newly released model that, with computer control tools off, took remote
  control of his OS and clicked a Create PR button during a GitHub outage.
- A proposed Congressional act would require agents and bots to declare their identity
  and intent, alongside related policy discussion from DEF CON's Policy Village.
- Eclipse Enclave, a sandbox for AI agents built on microVMs, makes constraints such as
  disabling network access much harder for an agent to bypass. Matthew Khouzam
  encountered it through the Sovereign AI Interest Group and offered to bring the
  maintainers in to present. By month end the group noted it gaining momentum as a
  heavier full-VM alternative to Nono-style sandboxing.
- Sal will present a six-minute demo of Nono, a runtime isolation project, to the chairs
  meeting in two weeks, showing how it lines up against the WG's protocols and
  primitives and where the gaps are. Govindaraj suggested a joint session with Identity
  and Trust and offered to host an interoperability workshop at the Dallas chapter.
- NVIDIA released an open source agent security platform, which the group discussed as
  capability brokering already appearing in frontier systems.
- Goose is refusing PRs against version 1.10 because version 2.0 rewrites the Rust and
  Electron architecture in TypeScript, so contributions should target 2.0.
- Govindaraj identified a FINOS project covering AI threat and risk mitigation and is
  surveying other LFX projects on AI and agent security so the WG can reuse existing
  assets.
- UTD and SMU in Dallas expressed interest in contributing researchers and PhD students,
  and Govindaraj is onboarding a UTD professor around MCP and WebMCP security. At the
  AAIF community meeting on 2026-09-26 he redirected a team building observability and
  security monitoring toward existing AAIF projects.
- A2A joined AAIF, and WG members may participate in A2A discussions as logistics are
  worked out.
- Industry news covered OpenAI declining to renew its contracts with Cursor following
  its acquisition via SpaceX, NVIDIA acquiring Hugging Face for $13 billion, and Apple
  seeing unexpected corporate demand for its machines as unified memory enables local
  LLM use.

## Blockers and Risks

- The cross-WG review checklist depends on a security and privacy delegate from every
  working group. Coverage is strong for Identity and Trust, Observability and Tracing,
  and Accuracy and Reliability, where WG members already participate. Agentic Commerce,
  Governance, and Workflow and Integration have no delegate engaged, and the chairs are
  raising the gap in the chairs meeting so the checklist can proceed with full
  representation.
- With the working groups now open to everyone, the WG is receiving one-off and
  AI-generated contributions from outside contributors. One individual opened more than
  75 PRs across AAIF repositories, and roughly 40 were dropped after Matthew's direct
  outreach. The chairs are drafting an AI contribution policy, the group is weighing an
  AI reviewer for first-pass feedback on incoming PRs, and the chairs' current
  preference is that contributors discuss a problem in a meeting before opening a PR.
- Review cycle time is the dominant cost in the WG's delivery process. Matthew's timing
  analysis shows authoring and reviewing each take little time and waiting for review
  takes most of it; a fix for the broken taxonomy build took three weeks because each
  comment round trip took about three days. The WG is delegating PR approval to
  workstream leads for their own folders, and Alex has asked to be pinged on Discord
  when reviews stall since GitHub notifications for the AAIF org were not reaching him.
- The Threat Modeling Gap Analysis merged on 2026-09-30, late against its 2026-07-01
  charter milestone, and dedicated workstream sessions only begin in October. Follow-on
  framework design work has no schedule yet.
- Participation is uneven. A small set of members does most of the work, new attendees
  arrive without context, and one review checklist meeting drew only AI notetakers.
  Govindaraj proposed a checkpoint summary of where each workstream stands against the
  charter scope, and Fernando cautioned that thin participation makes deliverables
  reflect only the lead's view.
- Scope delineation across working groups remains open as AAIF admits new projects. The
  TC confirmed on the August report that newly admitted projects such as A2A are in
  scope, and the chairs are taking the remaining question of how admissions are
  reflected in working group boundaries to the wg-chairs call.
- General availability remains a standing risk.
