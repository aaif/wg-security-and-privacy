# [Report] Security & Privacy – September 2026

**Working Group:** WG Security & Privacy

**Reporting period:** September 2026

**Chair(s):** Alexander Frazer (Runlayer) & Junjie Bu (Google)

**Date submitted:** 2026-09-30

---

## Summary

In September the working group brought its first deliverable to the finish line. The
Best Practices Guide reached its freeze-and-merge decision and a comment cutoff for the
first version, and Erin Farr's Security Best Practices Feature Matrix merged as the first
best practices artifact on `main`. The WG's security and privacy terms went up for review
in the cross-WG taxonomy repository, and the review checklist workstream is working to
bring a delegate from every working group to the table.

## Progress Against Objectives

- **Agentic AI Security Best Practices Guide** (Fernando Lucktemberg, Matthew Khouzam) —
  The WG decided to freeze, merge, and release the first version of the guide, then
  socialize it internally and promote it publicly as the group's first deliverable.
  Identity and trust topics are excluded from this version pending ownership discussion
  with the Identity and Trust WG. PR #9 is in final review with roughly 88 comments;
  the group set a comment cutoff, with simple fixes applied directly and larger items
  converted to referenced GitHub issues (for example issue #23 on workload identity
  guidance for multi-cloud and hybrid deployments). The merge is pending. Erin's
  Security Best Practices Feature Matrix, which maps each practice to the people,
  process, and technology features required to support it, merged via PR #20. Erin is
  also converting the practices into rules usable by static analysis tools and
  comparing the guide against major SDK security guidance, starting with the Google
  ADK.
- **Agentic AI Threat Modeling Gap Analysis and Framework Design** (Alon Mazor, Fernando
  Lucktemberg) — PR #8 remains open with review comments outstanding. Fernando is
  sequencing the work behind the Best Practices Guide merge and will alternate between
  the two afterward. He is also looking for a morning slot so APJ members can join the
  workstream meeting.
- **Security and Privacy Design Patterns Catalog** (Dan Kommatas, Sagar Dashora) — The
  kill-switch design patterns were added to the catalog and are under review by Aditya
  Gidh and Aileen Schultz, with memory-related patterns planned next. No PR from the
  leads has reached the repository yet. An external Attested Isolated Runtime draft
  (PR #11) from a taxonomy participant needs triage to confirm whether it belongs to
  this workstream.
- **Cross-Working Group Security and Privacy Review Checklist** (Govindaraj Palanisamy)
  — Scope overlap issues for each working group (#12 through #18) are open on the
  project board, working group leader contacts have been identified, and a cross-group
  meeting invitation with an availability poll is being sent. Security and Privacy
  members already cover Identity and Trust and Observability and Tracing well, and the
  chairs are working through the chairs meeting to bring delegates from the remaining
  groups on board. Junjie is following up with LF staff on project board permissions so
  issues can be assigned to sub-workstream leads.
- **Taxonomy / Landscape** (cross-WG, delegates Jautau White and Katherine Druckman) —
  Katherine's PR adding the Security and Privacy terms opened in the cross-WG repository
  on 2026-09-01 and is under review, with discussion on whether "Privacy Preserving
  Execution" should also cover sensitive and confidential data beyond privacy data. The
  workstream closed roughly 10-15 definitions across five PRs mid-month and adopted a
  merge-after-quiet-period process. Matthew shipped a taxonomy browser that lets
  contributors draft definitions with their own AI provider and records the LLM used
  and the human edit distance in the resulting PR.

## Ecosystem Highlights

- ETSI TC SAI chartered a new work item on cybersecurity requirements for AI services
  and systems, extending an existing European Norm to cover AI agents, with work
  progressing over the next year. Hsiao-Ying Lin introduced this WG's work there and
  asked whether a formal bidirectional information exchange can be established between
  the two bodies.
- OpenAI, Google, and Anthropic announced a stronger focus on testing and safety, which
  the group received with some skepticism given the pace of model releases. A member
  recounted a newly released model that, with computer control tools off, took remote
  control of his OS and clicked a Create PR button during a GitHub outage.
- A proposed Congressional act would require agents and bots to declare their identity
  and intent, alongside related policy discussion from DEF CON's Policy Village.
- Eclipse Enclave, a sandbox for AI agents built on microVMs rather than Docker, makes
  constraints such as disabling network access much harder for an agent to bypass.
  Matthew Khouzam encountered it through the Sovereign AI Interest Group and offered to
  bring the maintainers in to present.
- A2A joined AAIF, and WG members may participate in A2A discussions while logistics
  are worked out.
- Industry news covered OpenAI declining to renew its contracts with Cursor following
  its acquisition via SpaceX, NVIDIA acquiring Hugging Face for $13 billion, and Apple
  seeing unexpected corporate demand for its machines as unified memory enables local
  LLM use.

## Blockers and Risks

- The cross-WG review checklist depends on a security and privacy delegate from every
  working group, and several groups do not yet have one engaged. Coverage is strong
  where WG members already participate in other groups, and the chairs are raising the
  remaining gaps in the chairs meeting so the checklist can proceed with full
  representation.
- With the working groups now open to everyone, the WG is receiving one-off and
  AI-assisted contributions from outside contributors and has no agreed process yet for
  triaging them. This affects review load on the Best Practices Guide and the taxonomy
  repository, and the chairs are taking the question to the chairs meeting.
- Two deliverables are at risk against their charter targets. The Design Patterns
  Catalog has no content in the repository ahead of its 2026-10-01 target, and the
  Threat Modeling Gap Analysis is past its 2026-07-01 milestone and sequenced behind
  the Best Practices Guide.
- Scope delineation across working groups remains open as AAIF admits new projects. The
  TC confirmed on the August report that newly admitted projects such as A2A are in
  scope, and the chairs are taking the remaining question of how admissions are
  reflected in working group boundaries to the wg-chairs call.
- General availability remains a standing risk.

## Decisions Needed from the TC

- The WG requests a decision on whether it may establish a formal bidirectional
  information exchange with ETSI TC SAI on cybersecurity requirements for AI agents.
- The WG requests TC support in establishing a named security and privacy delegate from
  each working group for the cross-WG review checklist, so the checklist can operate as
  the intersecting layer the charter describes.
