# [Report] Security & Privacy – November 2026

**Working Group:** WG Security & Privacy

**Reporting period:** November 2026

**Chair(s):** Alexander Frazer (Runlayer) & Junjie Bu (Google)

**Date submitted:** 2026-11-30

---

## Summary

November was an intake month. Seven pull requests arrived, six of them from five people
opening their first PR in this repository, and the first contributed correction merged
into the published Best Practices Guide. A proposed control surfaces profile for agent
harnesses opened a wider discussion that exposed the kill switch and delegation as seams
no working group owns, which the chair took to the chairs meeting together with a charter
review. The APAC meeting welcomed first-time attendees from Tokyo and Shanghai, and Dawn
Wages joined the AAIF as Technical Program Manager.

## Progress Against Objectives

- **Agentic AI Security Best Practices Guide** (Fernando Lucktemberg, Matthew Khouzam) —
  Charter target 2026-10-01, met in September. PR #42 from Imran Siddique merged on
  2026-11-07, corrected the confidential-computing credential exposure row, and closed
  issue #37, which makes it the first contributed change to land in the published
  [guide](https://github.com/aaif/wg-security-and-privacy/blob/main/deliverables/DRAFT-agentic-ai-security-best-practices-guide.md).
  Three new guide PRs arrived: DeepTeam references for the evaluations section (#47, Matt
  Spiekerman), Sigstore and C2PA publisher provenance for skills (#49, `mrappard`), and
  capability-based pre-deployment evaluation with a definition of adversarial security
  tools (#52, Gurvinder Singh). Matthew opened #51, a non-blocking GitHub Action that
  checks the feature matrix against the guide and puts the first automated check on the
  deliverable. Neither lead attended the two workstream sessions before 2026-11-10, and
  the daylight saving change put the slot in conflict with Matthew's schedule, so the
  series moved to 9:30 AM Eastern from 2026-11-23. Matthew reviewed #47 and approved #49
  that day. The five October PRs (#36, #38 through #41) still await lead review.
- **Agentic AI Threat Modeling Gap Analysis and Framework Design** (Alon Mazor, Fernando
  Lucktemberg) — Charter target 2026-07-01. The
  [gap analysis](https://github.com/aaif/wg-security-and-privacy/blob/main/deliverables/DRAFT-agentic-ai-threat-modeling-gap-analysis.md)
  remains Draft v0.1. Fernando returned on 2026-11-10 after two and a half weeks away,
  scheduled the first framework design session for 2026-11-16, and invited SAP colleagues
  with deeper threat modeling experience. Fernando was absent on 2026-11-24, so the
  outcome of that session is not yet recorded. Matt Spiekerman opened #48 with wording
  refinements, the first contributed PR against the gap analysis. The framework design
  phase has no published schedule.
- **Security and Privacy Design Patterns Catalog** (Dan Kommatas, Sagar Dashora) —
  Charter target 2026-10-01, met in September. No new patterns landed, and the leads were
  absent from both primary meetings. Sagar will join the APAC meeting going forward
  because that slot suits Sagar's schedule. Issues #27 and #29 and PR #28 remain open for
  triage. The Kill Switch pattern drew the month's longest discussion, covered under
  cross-WG seams below.
- **Control Surfaces for Agentic Harnesses (proposed)** (Alex Frazer) —
  [PR #45](https://github.com/aaif/wg-security-and-privacy/pull/45) drafts a profile for
  the lifecycle events and authorization checkpoints an agent harness exposes, composing
  OpenID AuthZEN, the Agent Plugins hook names, OCSF, and OAuth token exchange. Alex
  presented it on 2026-11-10 as a conversation starter that an existing workstream could
  pick up. The discussion leaned toward scoping it around PDP and PEP integration across
  the harness, gateway, identity, and sandbox surfaces, and Kevin Hammond supported that
  narrowing on the PR from the Identity & Trust side, with that group owning delegation
  semantics. The WG has not decided on adoption, and the PR awaits a decision on whether
  it moves to the proposed Agent Harness working group.
- **Cross-Working Group Security and Privacy Review Checklist** (Govindaraj Palanisamy)
  — Charter target 2026-06-01, no v0 draft yet. Francesco Marinoni Moretto opened
  [PR #50](https://github.com/aaif/wg-security-and-privacy/pull/50) on 2026-11-23 with
  the Agentic Commerce requirements map, the first draft input in the workstream folder.
  It lists the questions a reviewer can ask of a deliverable at each step of an agent
  purchase, and the Agentic Commerce WG has yet to review it. Francesco also sent a
  document listing what each AAIF working group is missing for Agentic Commerce, and the
  WG decided on 2026-11-24 that it goes into a PR as well. Govindaraj agreed to share the
  cross-WG coordination approach with other chairs. The 2026-11-23 workstream session was
  cancelled.
- **Taxonomy / Landscape** (cross-WG, delegates Jautau White and Katherine Druckman) —
  The cross-WG workstream cleared its review backlog and moved on to the debate terms,
  including what counts as an agent, what counts as a model, and whether guardrails need
  to be deterministic and mathematically provable. New term PRs are welcome now that the
  queue is empty.
- **Cross-WG seams and charter review** — The 2026-11-10 discussion found that the kill
  switch could reasonably live in Security & Privacy, Identity & Trust, or Accuracy &
  Reliability, and Francesco confirmed it is an unowned seam in the Agentic standards
  map. Alex took the seams, starting with the kill switch and delegation, and a charter
  review to the 2026-11-23 chairs meeting. The chairs in attendance agreed on the problem
  and weighed two paths: map every seam and assign owners, or realign the groups around
  the organizational units that six months of experience suggest. Attendance was light
  over Thanksgiving week, so the discussion continues at the next chairs meeting.
- **APAC meeting** — The 2026-11-16 session welcomed Nick Fujita of Spellguard from Tokyo
  and Yaya Xia of Ant Group from Shanghai. Alex confirmed the session is a standing
  commitment, with notes and Discord carrying work between the two alternating meetings.
  Members asked for APAC-friendly sessions in Agentic Commerce, Attestation & Evidence,
  Identity & Trust, and Governance & Risk. Alex raised this with the chairs, who worried
  about low attendance, and floated a single cross-WG APAC meeting that builds critical
  mass and splits later.

## Ecosystem Highlights

- The [Attestation & Evidence working group proposal](https://github.com/aaif/working-group-proposals/issues/6)
  moved forward, with Steven Mih drafting the initial charter and a kickoff held by
  mid-month. Muhammad Usama Sardar, who has worked on attestation for about nine years
  across the IETF RATS working group and the Confidential Computing Consortium
  attestation SIG, left a supporting comment and offered to help. Govindaraj was
  scheduled to present the observability and attestation evidence layer to the Agentic
  Commerce WG on 2026-11-18.
- A proposed Agent Harness working group opened its Discord channel on 2026-11-16 with a
  scope that lines up with PR #45. It has no meeting or charter yet.
- Jason Keirstead described an effort to define how a policy enforcement point and a
  policy decision point integrate across every agent runtime surface. OWASP's ACS work
  covers harnesses, MCP interceptors cover gateway calls, AuthZEN covers identity, and
  sandboxes have no standard. Four of the 25 gateways Jason surveyed support an external
  PEP, and the MCP interceptor work excludes inference. Jason sees this groundwork as the
  route to an AARM-compliant PEP.
- Ty Smith shared that CIGAR 14.2 shipped with an
  [agnostic context graph benchmark](https://github.com/hashgraph-online/context-graph-benchmark)
  covering security, privacy, and efficiency, and that a second benchmark for AI security
  harnesses is planned, starting with HOL Guard. HOL also released a
  [standards map](https://hol.org/standards/map) of standards bodies, their working
  groups, and their standards, with an open source MCP server and an
  [AAIF-focused view](https://hol.org/standards/map#map?entity=aaif). Ty and Francesco
  plan to combine it with Francesco's Agentic standards map.
- Francesco reported that banks need to support know-your-agent practices. New Wells
  Fargo customer rules took effect on 2026-11-24 that make customers fully responsible
  for their agents' actions and require revocation by phone or postal mail, and
  Gurvinder noted that Bank of America and United have published similar AI use rules.
- Govindaraj raised concerns that the Agent Gateway reference implementation assumes
  Kubernetes, lacks support for serverless functions, managed agent platforms, and SaaS
  platforms, and logs to Postgres without following the observability and tracing work.
  Fernando offered a comparison of the three cloud agent platforms.
- Govindaraj is organizing a full-day, in-person AAIF community event at Southern
  Methodist University in Dallas on a Saturday in the third week of January. It has
  grown to involve AAIF, several organizations, and multiple universities, with speakers
  from Google, AWS, and Solo.io and a PEP and PDP demonstration from EY and a UT Dallas
  student.
- Two recent AAIF members joined through the APAC meeting. Spellguard released the
  [Spellguard-1.0-8B](https://huggingface.co/Spellguard/Spellguard-1.0-8B) guard model
  with eval scripts, and Nick proposed a sandbox standard to evaluate products against
  and an open catalog of security datasets. Ant Group maintains Kata Containers and works
  closely with the Open Sandbox team.
- Erin Farr raised Artex, an open source penetration testing framework that its author
  pulled after attackers adopted it, which prompted an
  [FBI flash advisory](https://www.ic3.gov/CSA/2026/261113.pdf). Gurvinder reported at
  least four similar autonomous frameworks, and vendors are already writing detection
  signatures for them. Erin noted that the harness drives much of the impact.
- Muhammad uses formal analysis to find attacks and propose fixes, with about 80 CVEs in
  total, 20 published and 57 or more in progress. Gurvinder is mapping the recent Railway
  attack to the OpenSSF Scorecard to produce deployment pipeline guidance, and the two
  plan to collaborate.

## Blockers and Risks

- The kill switch and delegation have no accountable working group, and members asked
  for a charter review that states what each group covers. PR #45 and the Kill Switch
  pattern have no settled home until the chairs name owners or realign the groups.
- Review capacity trails contribution volume. Fourteen PRs are open as of 2026-11-25,
  the oldest from 2026-09-30, and workstream leads were away for parts of the month.
  New contributors waiting a long time for a first review is the risk to watch.

## Decisions Needed from the TC

- Provide a unified AAIF calendar that covers the meetings held outside the working
  groups, such as the Goose, MCP, and A2A project meetings, alongside the working group
  meetings. A single view enables cross-AAIF alignment and makes collisions visible,
  such as the Accuracy & Reliability meeting overlapping A2A. Members also asked that
  the calendar be available to agents through an MCP server.
